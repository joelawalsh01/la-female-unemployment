The caveat that matters most

These are survey estimates, and at tract level they are very noisy. A five-year ACS tract estimate rests on roughly 250 sampled housing units. Once you narrow to women, in the labor force, and unemployed, the underlying counts are often in the low dozens.

Of the 2,463 analyzable tracts, 2,393 have a defined coefficient of variation. Exactly one is at or below 15%, and 2,022 (84%) exceed 40% — the level at which Census Bureau guidance treats an estimate as unreliable. The median is 55%. The remaining 70 tracts estimate female unemployment at exactly 0%, which leaves a relative error undefined; that is an artefact of a zero point estimate, not evidence of precision.

So: don't read a single tract's rate as a precise number, and don't read two adjacent tracts as different. The patterns — the income gradient, the Antelope Valley and South LA concentrations — are trustworthy in a way individual values are not, because they pool hundreds of tracts. Dragging the max coefficient of variation slider keeps only well-measured tracts, but that is not a neutral filter: the CV of a proportion falls as the proportion rises, so filtering hard biases the surviving sample upward. Watch the median move as you drag it.

How the measure is built

Female unemployment rate = women aged 16+ who are unemployed ÷ women aged 16+ in the civilian labor force, summed across every age band of ACS table B23001 (sex by age by employment status).

The obvious shortcut, subject table S2301, publishes a ready-made female rate — but only for ages 20–64. Building from B23001 covers the full 16-and-over population and lets margins of error be aggregated properly. One trap: ages 16–64 split the labor force into Armed Forces vs Civilian, so the denominator comes off the Civilian: line, while ages 65+ have no Armed Forces line, so there In labor force: already is the civilian total. Getting that wrong silently drops everyone over 65.

Margins of error follow ACS General Handbook ch. 8: root-sum-of-squares for sums, counting only the largest zero-estimate cell; the proportion formula for the rate, falling back to the ratio formula when the radicand goes negative. Coefficient of variation = (MOE / 1.645) / estimate.

Five-year estimates are a 2020–2024 average spanning the pandemic shock and recovery, not a 2024 snapshot. ACS records sex as a binary item, so “women” means everyone the survey classified as female. Twenty-five tracts have no women in the civilian labor force at all (jails, hospital campuses, industrial and port land). The two Channel Islands tracts are omitted from the map geometry but remain in the data.
